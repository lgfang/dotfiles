* Why billiard balls always separate at a 90° angle

A cue ball strikes a target ball of equal mass. Before the collision, the cue
ball travels with velocity $\vec{v}_0$. After the collision, the cue ball
deflects with velocity $\vec{v}_1$ and the target ball moves off with velocity
$\vec{v}_2$. The angle $\theta$ between $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$ is what we
want to determine.

\begin{tikzpicture}[thick]
  \coordinate (O) at (0, 0);
  \coordinate (T) at (3.61, 0);       % collision point
  \coordinate (V1) at (6.11, 1.66);   % end of v1
  \coordinate (V2) at (4.72, -1.66);  % end of v2

  % cue ball incoming: v0
  \draw[->] (O) -- (T) node[midway, below] {$\vec{v}_0$};

  % after collision: v1 and v2 from collision point
  \draw[->] (T) -- (V1) node[midway, above left] {$\vec{v}_1$};
  \draw[->] (T) -- (V2) node[midway, below right] {$\vec{v}_2$};

  % collision point
  \fill (T) circle (0.05);

  % angle theta between v1 and v2
  \draw (4.03,0.28) arc[start angle=33.69, end angle=-56.31, radius=0.5] node[midway, right] {$\theta$};
\end{tikzpicture}

NOTE: the proof below assumes an *elastic collision* (kinetic energy is
conserved) and *neglects friction*.

1. Conservation of Momentum:

   $$m\vec{v}_0 = m\vec{v}_1 + m\vec{v}_2$$

   Since the masses $m$ are equal and non-zero, cancel $m$:

   $$\vec{v}_0 = \vec{v}_1 + \vec{v}_2$$

   This vector sum forms a closed triangle where $\vec{v}_0$ is the resultant
   vector, and $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$ are the legs.

   \begin{tikzpicture}[thick,scale=1.5]
     \coordinate (O) at (0, 0);
     \coordinate (B) at (2.50, 1.66);    % vertex above v0
     \coordinate (T) at (3.61, 0);       % right end of v0

     % triangle sides
     \draw[->] (O) -- (B) node[midway, below] {$\vec{v}_1$};
     \draw[->] (B) -- (T) node[midway, right] {$\vec{v}_2$};
     \draw[->] (O) -- (T) node[midway, below] {$\vec{v}_0$};

     % angle theta at B (between v1 and v2)
     \draw (2.08,1.38) arc[start angle=213.69, end angle=303.69, radius=0.5] node[midway, below] {$180^\circ - \theta$};
   \end{tikzpicture}

2. Conservation of Kinetic Energy:

   $$\frac{1}{2}m\vert{}\vec{v}_0\vert{}^2 = \frac{1}{2}m\vert{}\vec{v}_1\vert{}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m\vert{}\vec{v}_2\vert{}^2$$

   Cancel $\frac{1}{2}m$:

   $$\vert{}\vec{v}_0\vert{}^2 = \vert{}\vec{v}_1\vert{}^2 + \vert{}\vec{v}_2\vert{}^2$$

3. Application of the Converse of the Pythagorean Theorem:

   By the Converse of the Pythagorean Theorem, a triangle whose sides satisfy
   $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ is a right triangle. Therefore, the angle between the
   velocity vectors $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$ after the collision is exactly
   90 degrees, i.e. $\vec{v}_1 \perp \vec{v}_2$.
